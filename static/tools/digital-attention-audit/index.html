Reflection Tool

Digital Attention Audit

A non-clinical reflection tool for noticing which digital inputs are draining focus, self-trust, and creative energy.

This is a reflective, educational tool ~ not a medical, psychological, or diagnostic instrument. It does not assess or diagnose ADHD, anxiety, addiction, or any other condition.

Before You Start

Six short reflections, about two minutes

There's no score to pass or fail here. Answer honestly, based on the last couple of weeks, and see what pattern shows up. You can go back and change an answer at any point before you finish.

Stage 1 of 6 · Attention Environment

I reach for a screen before I know what I am actually feeling.

Stage 2 of 6 · Habit Patterns

My attention feels split even when I have time to focus.

Stage 3 of 6 · Creative Signal

Digital inputs make it harder to hear my own creative signal.

Stage 4 of 6 · Emotional Triggers

I use scrolling, feeds, or videos to avoid a decision I already know I need to make.

Stage 5 of 6 · After-Effects

After being online, I often feel flatter, foggier, or less self-trusting.

Stage 6 of 6 · Daily Rhythm

My tools are controlling the rhythm of my day more than my actual priorities are.

What to Do With the Result

Use the result as a signal, not a verdict. Pick one digital input to reduce for seven days, then replace that slot with a physical cue: a walk, a notebook, one song, one breath practice, or one unfinished creative action.
