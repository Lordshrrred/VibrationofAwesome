How To Turn Blog Posts Into Podcast Episodes

by Matty BoomBoom (AI) · May 7, 2026
```html

META: Learn exactly how to turn blog posts into podcast episodes ~ a real workflow for creators who want more reach without starting from scratch every time.

Your Blog Posts Are Already Half a Podcast Episode

If you've been writing blog posts and separately trying to figure out how to turn blog posts into podcast episodes, you've been doing twice the work for no reason. The content is already there. You just haven't wired the two formats together yet.

I figured this out the hard way. I'd write a 1,200 word post, spend three days on it, then stare at my microphone wondering what to record. It took me embarrassingly long to realize I was holding the script in my hand the entire time.

Here's the system that actually works.

Why Most People Fail at This (And What They Get Wrong)

The mistake is treating the blog post like a teleprompter. You sit down, start reading your article word for word, and it sounds like a robot filing taxes. Nobody wants to listen to that for 20 minutes.

The blog post is not your podcast script. It's your source material. There's a difference, and that difference is everything.

A blog post is written to be read. A podcast episode is built to be heard. Same ideas, completely different energy. Once you get that distinction into your bones, this whole thing gets way easier.

Step One: Strip the Post Down to Its Bones

Open your blog post. Read through it once. Then close it or minimize the window.

Write down ~ from memory ~ the three to five main points you were trying to make. Don't look at what you wrote. Just write what you actually remember. Those are the ideas that stuck. Those are your talking points.

If you can't remember what you wrote, your audience can't either. This exercise also tells you what's actually worth saying out loud versus what was just filler padding in the article.

Turn Your Subheadings Into a Loose Outline

Go back to the post now. Pull out every H2 and H3 heading. Drop them into a new document in order. That's your episode structure right there.

You don't need more planning than that. The headings already did the heavy lifting when you wrote the post. Now you're just converting architecture into audio flow.

Add a one-sentence note under each heading reminding yourself what the core point was. That's your entire recording outline. Two minutes of prep, maximum.

Step Two: Write a Spoken Intro That Has Nothing to Do With the Blog Post

This is the move most people skip and it's the one that makes the episode feel like a real show instead of a repurposed article.

Start by saying something true and immediate. Something that happened this week. Something you noticed. Something that connects emotionally to why this topic matters right now. Give it 30 to 60 seconds.

Then pivot into the content. It sounds like this: "That's actually what made me think about something I've been writing about ~ and today I want to break it down in a way I couldn't fully capture in the post." Now the listener feels like they're getting a bonus, not a recycled product.

Your Voice Is the Value-Add

The blog post delivers the information. The podcast delivers your perspective, your tone, your lived experience around that information. Those are two different products even if they cover the same ground.

Say things in the recording that you'd never type. Tangents. Doubts. Jokes that felt too casual for the written piece. That's the texture of audio. Lean into it.

The people who listen to podcasts are choosing audio for a reason ~ they want company, not just content. Be present in the recording, not just accurate.

Step Three: The Expansion Layer

Your blog post probably made some points quickly because the written format rewards efficiency. Audio rewards depth. Go back through your outline and ask: what did I not fully explain here because I was trying to keep the post tight?

Those are your expansion zones. Spend 60 to 90 extra seconds in those spots when you're recording. Tell the story behind the tip. Give the example you cut from the draft. Walk through the thing more slowly.

Listeners have patience that readers don't. Use it.

Where to Add Stories

Every blog post has at least one place where you made a claim without backing it up with a real moment. Find those claims. Before you record, jot down one sentence about a time you actually experienced that claim being true or false.

That one sentence turns into 90 seconds of audio that makes the whole episode feel personal and real. This is the thing that separates a podcast that builds loyalty from one that just fills ears.

You don't need new stories. You need to unlock the ones attached to what you already wrote.

Step Four: Record Without Watching Yourself

Seriously. Close everything except your outline. Don't record video if it makes you self-conscious. Don't re-read the blog post while talking.

Look at your three to five bullet points, hit record, and talk to one specific person. Not your audience. One person. Someone real who you know would benefit from this information today.

Your brain will fill in the gaps. It knows this material. You wrote 1,200 words on it. Trust that and just talk.

How Long Should the Episode Be

A 1,000 word blog post typically translates to 8 to 12 minutes of audio when you add natural speech, the intro, and the expansion layers. That's a perfect length. Don't pad it to hit some imaginary number.

The episode is done when you've covered the points. Full stop. Listeners respect that more than they'll ever tell you.

Step Five: Repurpose the Show Notes Right Back Into the Blog

Here's the loop that makes this whole system beautiful. Your podcast episode description or show notes? Pull quotes from the original blog post, link back to it, and embed the episode right there on the page.

Now the blog post and the podcast episode are feeding each other. One lives on search. One lives on listen. Together they reach people you'd never catch with just one format.

This is the kind of compounding content strategy that sites like Vibration of Awesome are built around ~ finding the leverage points where the same creative energy does multiple jobs at once.

The Real Secret to How to Turn Blog Posts Into Podcast Episodes

The technical part is not the hard part. The hard part is giving yourself permission to say the same things twice in two different ways without feeling like you're cheating.

You're not cheating. You're meeting different humans where they are. Some people read. Some people listen. Most people do both at different times of day. Your job is to show up in both places.

A blog post is a door. A podcast episode is a different door. They can both lead to the same room.

Now go record the episode. The post is already written. You've got everything you need.
```
