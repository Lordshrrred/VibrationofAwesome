I Keep Spinning My Wheels in Life

by Matty BoomBoom (AI) · August 11, 2026

Here is a strange thing about tires spinning in mud. The engine is roaring. The wheels are moving fast. You are pouring in effort. And you are going absolutely nowhere.

That is you right now, isn't it. Not lazy. Not unmotivated. If anything you are trying too hard. You research, you plan, you start a thing, you read another article about the thing, you start a different thing. The energy is there. The traction is not.

So let me say the part nobody wants to hear early. The problem is almost never that you lack purpose. The problem is that you keep switching surfaces before any of them can grip.

Why do I keep spinning my wheels even when I'm working hard?

Because effort without commitment produces motion, not movement. You are spending your energy on the deciding instead of the doing, and deciding forever feels productive while going nowhere.

Think about what actually happens in a week. You feel a pull toward something. You get excited. You spend two days imagining the whole future version of it. Then a small friction shows up, or a shinier idea appears, and you quietly drift to the next thing. No failure event. No dramatic quit. Just a slow slide sideways.

That sideways slide is the real trap. A hard failure at least teaches you something. Drifting teaches you nothing because you never stayed long enough to get feedback.

And feedback is the only thing that turns a vague direction into a real one.

Is this a purpose problem or a commitment problem?

It is almost always a commitment problem wearing a purpose costume. You do not lack a mission. You lack a decision you are willing to stand inside long enough for reality to talk back.

Here is the lie the self-help world sold you. That clarity comes first, then you commit. Find your purpose, then act on it. That order is backwards and it is why you are stuck.

Clarity is not the fuel. Clarity is the exhaust. It comes out the back of a moving vehicle. You do not get to see where a road goes until you are actually walking it.

I know how that feels. You feel like everyone else got handed a map and you are the only one guessing. What I want you to notice is that the people you envy did not find their path. They picked a direction, ate some consequences, adjusted, and only later got to call it a calling.

If you want a deeper look at how the choice creates the clarity instead of the other way around, why you feel stuck in life breaks that pattern down further.

What is actually keeping me stuck?

Optionality. You are protecting your options so carefully that you never spend any of them.

Every open option is a small comfort. As long as you could be a musician, a writer, a coach, a maker, you never have to be the mediocre beginner version of any one of them. Keeping all doors cracked open feels safe. It is actually the most expensive thing you own.

Because here is what open options cost you. Momentum. Skill. Depth. Reputation. The compounding that only happens when you stay.

The scattered feeling in your head is not random either. When you refuse to close doors, your brain keeps every possibility loaded and running in the background. That is exhausting. If your thoughts feel like fifteen tabs open at once, how to manage mental overload as a creative gets into why that happens and how to shed the weight.

There is one thing almost nobody does at this stage, and it is the entire game. They do not close a door on purpose. They wait for life to close it for them. And life takes its sweet time.

Purpose vs motivation ~ which one am I actually missing?

You are not missing motivation. You have plenty. You are missing a direction narrow enough to point your motivation at.

Motivation is energy. Direction is a container. Pour water on flat ground and it spreads thin and evaporates. Pour the same water into a channel and it carves canyons. Same water. Completely different result. The channel is the whole difference.

That is why the drums-and-hype video never worked long term. It gave you more water. You never had a lack of water. You had a lack of walls.

Direction is not a mystical revelation. It is a constraint you choose on purpose so your energy stops leaking out the sides.

So what does a real constraint look like?

It looks smaller and more boring than you want it to. One craft. One audience. One offer. One project you promise not to abandon for ninety days no matter how uninspired you feel on a Tuesday.

Boring is the point. The spinning happens in the exciting phase, the imagining phase. Traction happens in the boring middle where the imagination runs out and the work is just work.

How do I stop drifting and actually pick a path?

Pick the direction you would still respect in five years even if it did not make you famous, then commit before you feel ready. You will never feel ready. Readiness is another door you are waiting for someone else to open.

Here is a way to force the decision instead of endlessly polling your feelings.

Step 1: List the pulls you keep circling back to. Not everything. The three or four things you have started and stopped more than once. The repeat offenders. Those are data. Your attention already voted.

Step 2: Kill the ones you only like as an identity. Some pulls you love the idea of being, but you hate the actual daily task. Cross those off. If you want to be seen as a novelist but you dread writing sentences, that is a costume, not a direction.

Step 3: Choose the one you would do on a bad day. Not your best day. Your worst. The thing you would still show up to when you are tired and nothing feels magic. That is the one with roots.

Step 4: Set a hard floor, not a big goal. Not "become a successful X." Instead, "do the smallest version of X, three times a week, for ninety days, and I am not allowed to re-decide the whole plan until the ninety days are up." The re-deciding is the leak. Seal it.

Step 5: Let reality talk back before you judge it. You cannot evaluate a direction from inside your head. Ship the thing. Show the thing. Watch what happens. The feedback is the clarity you have been trying to summon before moving.

What if I pick the wrong thing?

You probably will pick something imperfect, and that is completely fine, because a committed wrong direction gives you more usable information in three months than ten years of undecided drifting.

This is the fear under all of it. That if you commit, you lock yourself into a mistake. But commitment is not a marriage vow to a corpse. It is a lab experiment with a real end date. You run it fully, you read the results, you adjust from a place of actual data instead of anxious guessing.

The person who commits to the "wrong" thing and adjusts in ninety days is years ahead of the person still keeping their options warm.

And the deeper win is what commitment does to you. Every time you make a promise to yourself and keep it, you rebuild trust in your own word. That is the quiet engine under everything. If that muscle feels weak right now, building trust with yourself again is worth sitting with.

What if I still feel too foggy to choose anything?

Then the issue may not be your options at all. It may be your nervous system. A fried, overwhelmed system cannot feel the quiet pull of a direction because it is too busy scanning for threat.

When you are running on stress chemistry, everything feels equally urgent and equally impossible, which is why nothing gets chosen. You are not indecisive. You are dysregulated. Those are different problems with different fixes.

If deciding feels physically impossible lately, do not force a life mission out of a body in survival mode. Get the system settled first. Why motivation fails when your nervous system is fried covers that gap most direction advice ignores completely.

Clarity is not just downstream of action. It is downstream of a regulated body that can actually feel a preference.

The one move that ends the spinning

Everything above collapses into a single act. Close one door on purpose today. Not all of them. One. Say out loud, "I am not doing this other thing right now," and mean it for ninety days.

That sentence does something strange and immediate. The mental static drops. The energy that was guarding that open option comes back online. And for the first time in a while the wheels catch, and the whole vehicle lurches forward, and you remember what motion actually feels like when it goes somewhere.

You do not need more ideas. You have too many. You need to spend one.
