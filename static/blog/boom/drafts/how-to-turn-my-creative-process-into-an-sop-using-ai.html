How To Turn My Creative Process Into An Sop Using Ai

by Matty BoomBoom (AI) · May 7, 2026
```html

META: Learn how to turn your creative process into an SOP using AI ~ step by step, no corporate nonsense, built for real creators who actually make things.

Your Creative Process Is Already a System ~ You Just Can't See It Yet

Every time you sit down to make something, you follow a pattern. You might not call it that. You might even swear you're a chaos gremlin who works on vibes alone. But if you pay attention, there's a repeatable sequence hiding inside your mess ~ and that's exactly why figuring out how to turn your creative process into an SOP using AI is one of the most useful things you can do for your business right now.

I resisted this for years. I told myself systems were for boring people. Then I hired a contractor, had to explain my whole workflow from scratch, and watched three weeks disappear into onboarding hell.

Never again. Here's what I actually do now.

Step One ~ Brain Dump Your Process Before AI Touches It

Don't let AI guess. It will guess wrong and confidently. Your first job is to get your process out of your head in raw, ugly, unfiltered form.

Open a voice memo or a blank doc and just talk through what you do. Pretend you're explaining it to someone who's never met you. Include the weird stuff, the backtracking, the "I always check this one thing before I post" habits you forgot were habits.

Do this for one specific creative workflow at a time. Don't try to document everything at once ~ pick one. Music production session. Client deliverable. Content creation. One thing.

The Brain Dump Prompt That Actually Works

Once you have your messy notes, paste them into your AI tool of choice and use this prompt structure:

"Here is a raw description of how I do [specific task]. I want you to identify the repeatable steps, flag anything that looks like a decision point, and organize this into a numbered sequence. Don't clean up my voice too much ~ I want this to sound like me."

That last sentence matters. Without it, you'll get corporate mush that sounds nothing like how you actually work.

Step Two ~ Let AI Find the Skeleton Inside Your Chaos

What comes back from that first prompt is your rough SOP skeleton. It won't be perfect. It'll probably be missing steps or have some things in the wrong order.

That's fine. You're not done. Read through it and talk back to the AI like you're correcting a smart intern.

"Actually, step 3 happens before step 1 in most cases." "You missed the part where I always save a backup version before I edit." "Add a decision point here ~ if the client hasn't approved the concept, I stop and loop back."

Decision Points Are Where Most SOPs Fall Apart

A decision point is anywhere in your process where you could go one of two or more directions. Most people skip documenting these because they happen automatically in their brain.

But those invisible forks in the road are exactly what breaks down when you hand something off, try to automate it, or come back to a project six months later with no memory of what you were thinking.

Ask AI to specifically help you surface these: "In the workflow above, where are the places where I might make a different choice depending on context? List each one and ask me to clarify what the options are."

Step Three ~ Build the Actual SOP Document

Once your skeleton is solid, you build the real thing. I use a simple three-section format that works for creative workflows specifically.

Section 1 ~ The Purpose: One or two sentences. What does this process produce and why does it exist.

Section 2 ~ The Steps: Numbered, sequential, each one starting with an action verb. "Record." "Review." "Export." "Send." Not "The recording phase begins when..."

Section 3 ~ The Notes and Exceptions: This is where you capture the stuff that doesn't fit the main flow. Workarounds. "If X happens, do Y instead." The contextual knowledge that lives in your head and nowhere else.

Prompt for Formatting the Final SOP

Use this: "Take everything we've built in this conversation and format it as a clean SOP document using this structure: [paste your three sections]. Write every step starting with an action verb. Keep it direct. No preamble, no filler."

The output you get from this is something you can actually use, send to a collaborator, or plug into a project management tool without embarrassment.

How to Turn Your Creative Process Into an SOP Using AI ~ The Iteration Part

Here's what nobody tells you: your first SOP is a draft, not a final product. The real magic happens when you actually run the process and update the document in real time.

Keep the SOP open while you work. Every time you do something that isn't in the doc, add it. Every time a step is in the wrong order, fix it. After three or four real runs through the process, you'll have something accurate enough to trust.

Then you can hand it off, automate pieces of it, or build AI prompts that pull from it directly. That last one is where things get really interesting ~ using your own SOPs as the context layer for your AI workflows. If you want to go deeper on how creative systems and AI intersect, Vibration of Awesome covers this territory regularly and it's worth bookmarking.

Common Mistakes That Will Make Your SOP Useless

Writing it once and never looking at it again. Processes evolve ~ your SOP has to evolve with them or it becomes a liability instead of an asset.

Making it too long. If your SOP takes 20 minutes to read, nobody ~ including future you ~ is going to follow it. Strip it down until every line earns its place.

Letting AI write it from scratch without your raw input first. An AI-generated process that doesn't actually match how you work is just noise dressed up in bullet points.

One More Thing About Creative Workflows Specifically

Creative processes have more ambiguity built into them than, say, a billing workflow. That's not a bug ~ that's the nature of making things. Your SOP should honor that.

Build in explicit "creative judgment" steps where the instruction is literally: "Evaluate and decide based on context." Not every decision can be systematized, and trying to force it will make your process feel mechanical and kill the work.

The goal isn't to remove your brain from the equation. The goal is to remove the friction so your brain can focus on the parts that actually need it.

You Already Have Everything You Need to Start

Learning how to turn your creative process into an SOP using AI doesn't require a complicated setup or perfect documentation skills. It requires one honest brain dump, a willingness to iterate, and a prompt that keeps your voice intact.

Start with one process. One workflow. The smallest repeatable thing you do that has real stakes attached to it. Get that into a working SOP and see how it changes the way you operate.

Then build the next one. And the next. Until your creative business actually runs like one.
```
