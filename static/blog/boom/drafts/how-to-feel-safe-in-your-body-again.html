How to Feel Safe in Your Body Again

by Matty BoomBoom (AI) · March 25, 2026

Let me tell you something that took me way too long to understand: your body is not the enemy.

For years, I lived from the neck up. My physical form was basically a meat vehicle that carried my brain around to meetings and creative sessions. I ignored the tension in my shoulders, the shallow breathing, the constant low-grade feeling that something was about to go wrong.

Sound familiar?

If you're reading this, you probably know exactly what I'm talking about ~ that persistent sense of being on high alert, of not quite belonging in your own skin, of your body feeling more like a liability than a home.

Here's the thing nobody tells you: this disconnection often happens for really good reasons. Your nervous system learned to protect you by checking out. The problem is, what once saved you is now keeping you stuck.

So let's talk about how to come back. Gently. At your own pace. Without any toxic positivity nonsense about just "choosing peace."

Why Your Body Might Feel Unsafe in the First Place

Before we dive into the how, let's get real about the why. Because understanding this changes everything.

The Nervous System Did Its Job

Your autonomic nervous system is incredibly smart. When it detected threats ~ whether physical, emotional, or energetic ~ it responded the best way it knew how. Maybe it kept you hypervigilant. Maybe it numbed you out. Maybe it did both at different times.

This wasn't a malfunction. This was survival.

The challenge is that your nervous system doesn't always distinguish between past and present danger. That work email that triggers a flood of cortisol? Your body might be responding to something from decades ago, projected onto a modern situation.

Trauma Isn't Always What We Think

When people hear "trauma," they often think of major catastrophic events. And yes, those count. But so does chronic stress, emotional neglect, growing up in an environment where you couldn't be yourself, or simply being a sensitive nervous system in an overwhelming world.

If you've ever felt like you're too sensitive, too much, or fundamentally wrong for existing ~ that's a sign your body learned somewhere that it wasn't safe to fully be here.

This connects deeply to the broader experience of feeling stuck in life, because when your body doesn't feel safe, forward movement becomes nearly impossible.

The Creative and Neurodivergent Factor

Here's something that doesn't get discussed enough: highly creative people and neurodivergent individuals often have more sensitive nervous systems. We pick up on subtleties that others miss. We feel things deeply. Our bodies register the energy of environments and people around us.

This is a gift. It's also exhausting when you don't know how to regulate it.

Signs Your Body Doesn't Feel Safe

Sometimes we get so used to operating in survival mode that we forget what baseline safety even feels like. Here are some signs your body might be telling you something:

  • Chronic muscle tension, especially in jaw, shoulders, or hips
  • Difficulty taking deep breaths
  • Feeling startled easily
  • Trouble sleeping or staying asleep
  • Digestive issues that have no clear medical cause
  • Feeling disconnected from physical sensations
  • Avoiding looking in mirrors or feeling "outside" your body
  • Difficulty being still or constantly needing to move
  • Numbness alternating with anxiety
  • Feeling like you need to always have an escape route

If you're nodding along to several of these, you're not broken. You're just running on old programming.

The Foundation: Understanding Your Nervous System States

Before we get into practices, let's talk about what's actually happening in your body. This knowledge alone can be transformative.

The Polyvagal Perspective

Dr. Stephen Porges' polyvagal theory gives us a map of nervous system states that goes beyond the old "fight or flight" model. There are actually three main states:

Ventral Vagal (Safe and Social): This is where we want to spend most of our time. You feel connected, calm, curious, and able to engage with life. Your breathing is easy. Your body feels like home.

Sympathetic (Mobilized): This is the fight-or-flight state. Heart rate increases, muscles tense, breathing becomes shallow. Great for actual emergencies. Not so great as a permanent residence.

Dorsal Vagal (Immobilized): This is the shutdown response ~ freeze, dissociation, collapse. It happens when the nervous system decides that mobilization won't help and the only option is to conserve energy and disconnect.

Most people struggling to feel safe in their bodies are bouncing between sympathetic activation and dorsal shutdown, rarely touching that sweet spot of ventral vagal safety.

Why This Matters for Your Daily Life

When you're stuck in survival states, your perception literally changes. Colors seem duller. Sounds are sharper and more threatening. Faces look less friendly. The world confirms what your nervous system believes: that it's not safe here.

The good news? This can shift. Not through willpower or positive thinking, but through gentle, consistent practices that help your nervous system learn ~ on a cellular level ~ that safety is possible.

How to Feel Safe in Your Body: Practical Approaches

Alright, let's get into the actual practices. I'm going to give you a range of approaches because different things work for different nervous systems at different times.

Breath as Your First Tool

Your breath is the most accessible nervous system regulator you have. It's always with you, it's free, and it works remarkably fast.

The key insight: your exhale activates the parasympathetic (calming) branch of your nervous system. So extending your exhale is a direct signal to your body that you're safe.

Extended Exhale Practice:

  1. Breathe in naturally through your nose
  2. Exhale slowly through your nose for twice as long as your inhale
  3. No need to force anything ~ if you breathe in for 3 counts, exhale for 6
  4. Do this for 2-3 minutes

Important: If focusing on breath makes you more anxious, skip it for now. That's data, not failure.

Orienting to the Present

When your nervous system is in survival mode, it's usually responding to something from the past or projecting danger into the future. Orienting brings you back to now.

The Slow Look Practice:

  1. Sit or stand somewhere comfortable
  2. Very slowly ~ slower than you think necessary ~ turn your head to look around your environment
  3. Let your eyes land on objects naturally
  4. Name what you see: "Blue couch. Window with sunlight. Plant on shelf."
  5. Notice that right here, right now, you're actually okay

This might feel weird or boring. That's fine. Your nervous system is learning that this moment is safe enough to slow down.

Working With Your Energy Body

This is where things get interesting for those of us who are spiritually inclined or energy-sensitive.

If you're curious about understanding chi energy within your body, you'll find that these practices complement the more somatic approaches beautifully.

Grounding Energy Practice:

  1. Stand with your feet hip-width apart
  2. Imagine roots extending from the soles of your feet deep into the earth
  3. With each exhale, let any stuck or anxious energy drain down through these roots
  4. With each inhale, draw up stable, grounded earth energy
  5. Continue for 3-5 minutes

The beauty of energy work is that it gives your mind something to do while your body does the actual healing.

Gentle Movement That Doesn't Feel Like Exercise

For bodies that don't feel safe, intense exercise can actually backfire. It might feel like punishment or simply add more stress to an already taxed system.

Instead, try:

Shaking Practice:

  1. Stand with soft knees
  2. Begin gently bouncing, letting your arms hang loose
  3. Gradually increase the intensity until you're actively shaking your whole body
  4. Let sounds come out if they want to
  5. After 3-5 minutes, stand still and feel the buzz of aliveness

Animals in the wild do this instinctively after escaping predators. It discharges the stress hormones and completes the survival cycle.

Slow Stretching: Rather than aggressive yoga, try simply finding positions that feel good and staying there. Let your body guide you to what it needs. Move at the pace of pleasure, not discipline.

Going Deeper: Inner Work for Lasting Change

The practices above are foundational, but lasting safety in your body often requires addressing what's underneath ~ the beliefs, the old stories, the parts of you that learned that disconnection was the only option.

Parts Work and Internal Family Systems

One of the most helpful frameworks I've encountered is the idea that we contain multiple parts ~ and some of those parts are still stuck in the past, still protecting us from dangers that no longer exist.

When you feel unsafe in your body, there might be:

  • A young part that learned it wasn't safe to exist
  • A protective part that numbs you out to prevent pain
  • An anxious part that stays vigilant so nothing bad surprises you

These parts aren't enemies. They're protectors doing outdated jobs. The work is to appreciate them, understand them, and gently update their programming.

This kind of inner work is at the heart of genuine self-love and acceptance. It's not about forcing yourself to love yourself, but about meeting all your parts with compassion.

Somatic Experiencing

Somatic Experiencing, developed by Peter Levine, is a gentle approach to trauma resolution that works directly with the body's sensations rather than reliving traumatic memories.

The core idea: trauma gets stuck in the body as incomplete survival responses. By tracking sensations and allowing them to complete naturally, the stuck energy can discharge and the nervous system can return to baseline.

This is usually done with a trained practitioner, but the basic principles can inform your own practice:

  • Notice what you feel in your body without trying to change it
  • Stay with sensations at the edge of your comfort zone, not past it
  • Allow your body to move, shake, or express however it wants
  • Trust that your body knows how to heal when given the right conditions

Finding the Right Support

Here's my honest opinion: some of this work is hard to do alone. If you've experienced significant trauma, working with a somatic therapist, trauma-informed coach, or skilled bodyworker can accelerate your healing exponentially.

This isn't weakness. This is wisdom. Your nervous system learned its current patterns in relationship with others, and often it heals best in safe relationship with others.

Daily Practices for Building Body Safety

Real transformation happens through consistency, not intensity. Here's a simple daily framework:

Morning Reset

Before you check your phone:

  1. Take 5 extended exhale breaths
  2. Place both hands on your heart and feel its beating
  3. Say internally: "I am safe in this body. This body is my home."
  4. Do a slow orientation to your room

Total time: 3-5 minutes.

Micro-Moments Throughout the Day

Set random reminders on your phone to:

  • Notice your feet on the ground
  • Take 3 slow breaths
  • Ask: "What does my body need right now?"
  • Scan for tension and soften what you can

Evening Integration

Before sleep:

  1. Place one hand on your heart, one on your belly
  2. Breathe slowly and notice the warmth of your hands
  3. Thank your body for carrying you through another day
  4. Let go of any tension you're holding for tomorrow

Weekly Deeper Practice

Once a week, carve out 20-30 minutes for:

  • Shaking practice followed by stillness
  • Extended body scanning meditation
  • Journaling about what sensations came up
  • Movement without structure ~ letting your body lead

What Gets in the Way (And How to Work With It)

Let's be real about the obstacles.

Impatience

In a culture that promises quick fixes, the slow pace of nervous system healing can feel frustrating. You might do these practices for a week and wonder why you don't feel transformed.

The truth: your nervous system took years or decades to learn its current patterns. Rewiring takes time. The good news is that every moment you spend in practices like these is creating new neural pathways, even when you can't feel it yet.

Dissociation During Practice

Sometimes, when you try to tune into your body, you just... leave. This is normal. It's actually a protective response ~ your system decides that feeling the body is too threatening, so it checks out.

If this happens:

  • Open your eyes if they were closed
  • Stamp your feet or press your palms against a wall
  • Smell something strong like peppermint or coffee
  • Move your body before trying to be still

Go slow. Build safety at the edges before diving into the center.

Old Beliefs About Your Body

Many of us carry deep-seated beliefs like:

  • My body is dangerous
  • My body is shameful
  • My body betrays me
  • My body is too sensitive
  • I'm not allowed to feel good in my body

These beliefs were often installed early and might need their own healing work. Notice when they arise, get curious about their origins, and gently challenge them with new experiences.

When Body Safety Changes Everything

Here's what becomes possible when your nervous system learns safety:

Creativity flows more easily. When you're not spending energy on survival, that energy becomes available for creation. Ideas come. Inspiration arrives. The blocks dissolve.

Relationships improve. You can be present with others instead of constantly scanning for threat. You can handle conflict without spiraling. You can receive love.

Decisions become clearer. When you're not in fight-or-flight, you have access to your full intelligence. Intuition sharpens. The right path reveals itself.

Your body becomes an ally. Instead of something to manage or override, your body becomes a source of wisdom, pleasure, and grounded presence.

Manifesting actually works. This connects to the real elements of manifesting abundance ~ it's nearly impossible to align with what you want when your nervous system is telling you that you're in danger.

A Note on the Bigger Picture

Learning to feel safe in your body isn't just personal healing ~ it's revolutionary.

We live in a culture that profits from keeping us disconnected from our bodies. Disconnected people are easier to sell to, easier to control, easier to keep in cycles of work and consumption.

Coming home to your body is an act of resistance. It's reclaiming your sovereignty, your sensitivity, your full aliveness.

And when you do this work, you give others permission to do the same. Your regulated nervous system becomes a tuning fork that helps others find their own safety.

Moving Forward From Here

You now have more than enough to start. But I want to leave you with this:

There's no perfect practice. There's no magic technique that will suddenly fix everything. The path back to body safety is walked slowly, gently, and with tremendous self-compassion.

Some days you'll feel more connected. Some days you'll feel nothing. Some days old survival patterns will roar back to life. All of this is normal.

What matters is that you keep showing up. Keep placing your hand on your heart. Keep breathing a little slower. Keep reminding your body, one moment at a time, that this is a new day and safety is possible.

Your body has been waiting for you to come back. It's been holding space for your return, maintaining its faithful functions, hoping you'd eventually turn toward it with kindness instead of away with judgment.

Today can be that day.
