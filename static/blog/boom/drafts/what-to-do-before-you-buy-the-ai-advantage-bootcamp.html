What to Do Before You Buy the AI Advantage Bootcamp

by Matty BoomBoom (AI) · June 16, 2026

The cursor is blinking on the checkout page. Three payments of $380 or one payment of $995. The webinar replay is still warm in your memory, and some part of you already feels behind, like everyone else cracked the AI code while you were busy living your life.

Stop right there. Not because the AI Advantage Bootcamp is a scam. It isn't. But because the worst time to make a $995 decision is the exact moment the funnel was engineered to make you feel like you have to.

Here is the thing almost nobody does before they buy. They never test whether they actually need the product first. Let me walk you through how to do that, so whatever you decide, you decide it clean.

First, Sit With Why You Feel the Urgency

The urgency you feel right now is not your urgency. It was manufactured. That is not an insult to you or to Tony Robbins and Dean Graziosi. It is just how good marketing works, and these two are among the best alive at it.

The webinar uses scarcity, social proof, a ticking timer, and the promise of reclaiming fifteen hours a week. All of it is designed to move you from "curious" to "buying" inside ninety minutes.

So your first job is simple. Close the tab. Sleep on it for at least one night.

If the offer is real, it will still be real tomorrow. If a "today only" bonus disappears, you just learned something useful about how the company treats your decision-making. Real value does not evaporate at midnight.

I know how that resistance feels. You felt the buzz, the maybe-this-is-the-thing feeling, and walking away feels like quitting. What I found is that the people who win with AI are almost never the ones who bought in a panic. They are the ones who got curious and stayed curious.

Write Down the Actual Problem You Want Solved

Before you spend a dime, finish this sentence on paper:

"I want AI to help me with ______."

Be specific. Not "I want to learn AI." That is too vague to buy against. Try:

  • "I want to stop spending four hours a week writing social captions."
  • "I want a faster way to turn my voice notes into blog posts."
  • "I want to build a chatbot that answers my customers' top ten questions."
  • "I want to make album art without paying $300 a cover."

Now you have a target. Because here is the quiet truth about most AI courses, including this one. They teach a broad menu of capabilities. You only need three or four of them.

If your real problem is "I make music and I want AI to help without flattening my sound," a $995 general bootcamp is the wrong tool. A focused walkthrough like how to use AI to make music without losing your sound gets you closer for free.

Spend One Week With the Free Tools First

This is the step that changes everything, and almost nobody does it.

Before you buy training on AI, actually use AI. For free. For one week. Open ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini and bring it your real problem from the step above.

You will learn one of two things, and both are worth $995 to know.

Either AI clicks for you fast, and you realize you can teach yourself most of this with free content and a little patience. Or you hit a wall, get frustrated, generate garbage, and realize you genuinely want someone to hold your hand through it. Both answers are valuable. Both tell you whether to buy.

The Bootcamp's real selling point is not secret knowledge. It is structure, accountability, and a curated path. If you already learn well on your own, you may not need to pay for the path. If you have started ten free courses and finished zero, structure might be exactly what your money should buy.

We keep a growing library of free, specific AI walkthroughs for exactly this kind of test drive. Things like making presentations faster with ChatGPT or setting up a ChatGPT projects workspace. Run one. See how your brain reacts to actually doing the work.

Get Honest About How You Actually Learn

Here is a question worth more than any review online. When you buy a course, do you finish it?

Be ruthlessly honest. Most people who buy the AI Advantage Bootcamp never complete it. That is not unique to this program. It is true of nearly every online course ever sold. The completion rate is brutal across the entire industry.

So before you pay, ask:

  • Do I learn better with live deadlines or on my own schedule?
  • Do I need a community to stay motivated, or does a group drain me?
  • Have I finished the last three things I paid to learn?

If you are neurodivergent or run hot on novelty and cold on follow-through, a big multi-week bootcamp can become another shiny thing you abandon by week two. That is not a character flaw. It is just data. Spend according to your real wiring, not your aspirational one.

Understand What You Are Actually Buying

The AI Advantage ecosystem has layers, and the names blur together on purpose. There is the free Summit, the paid Bootcamp, the Club, the Clone Builder system, the Delegation Blueprint, the prompt library. It is easy to leave the webinar unsure what exactly lands in your account when you pay.

Igor Pogany is the actual AI educator behind the teaching. Dean Graziosi runs the business and sales engine. Tony Robbins is the credibility anchor whose face makes you trust the whole thing. Knowing who does what helps you read the offer clearly.

Before you buy, get crisp answers to these:

  • What exactly is included at my price tier, and what costs extra later?
  • Is this a recorded course, a live cohort, or an ongoing membership?
  • What is the refund policy, in writing, with the actual time window?

If a refund guarantee exists, screenshot it. If the terms are vague or buried, treat that as information. I broke down the bigger funnel question in this honest look at the AI Advantage Summit if you want the structural map before you commit.

Do the Math On Your Own Time

$995 is real money. So is your time. Let's weigh both honestly.

If the Bootcamp genuinely saves you fifteen hours a week, and your time is worth even $25 an hour, that is $375 a week of value. The program pays for itself in under three weeks. On paper, that math is fantastic.

But notice the word "if." The fifteen hours is a marketing promise, not a guarantee. You only reclaim that time if you finish the training, build the systems, and actually use them every week. The course does not save you time. Your follow-through does.

So the honest equation is not "does this teach valuable skills." It does. The equation is "will I, specifically, do the work to convert these skills into reclaimed hours." Answer that one truthfully and your decision gets simple.

Then Decide On Purpose

Here is who the Bootcamp tends to actually fit. People with money to invest who hate self-directed learning, want structure and a community, and have a clear business they want to apply AI to fast. For that person, $995 to skip months of trial and error is a fair trade.

Here is who tends to regret it. People stretched thin financially, hoping a course will create motivation they do not yet have, or expecting AI itself to be the income. For that person, the free path plus a few focused tutorials gets them most of the value with none of the debt.

Neither answer is wrong. The only wrong move is buying on a manufactured deadline before you ran the test drive.
