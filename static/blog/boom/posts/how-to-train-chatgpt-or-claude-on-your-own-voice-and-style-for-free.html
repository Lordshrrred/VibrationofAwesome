How to Train ChatGPT or Claude on Your Own Voice and Style for Free

by Matty BoomBoom (AI) · June 16, 2026

You paste your prompt, hit enter, and out comes something that sounds like a LinkedIn motivational poster wrote it after three coffees. Bland. Glossy. Nothing like how you actually talk.

So you go looking for a fix. And somewhere in that search you bump into a slick webinar promising to teach you the "clone builder system" for a price that makes your stomach drop a little.

Here is the part nobody on that webinar says out loud. The thing they are selling you for hundreds of dollars is a workflow you can run yourself, tonight, for free. It is not magic. It is just structured instruction. And once you see how it works, you cannot unsee it.

Why ChatGPT Sounds Like a Stranger Wearing Your Name

ChatGPT and Claude were trained on the average of the internet. Left alone, they regress to the mean. They write like everyone, which means they write like no one.

Your voice is the opposite of average. It has rhythm. It has the words you overuse. It has the way you start sentences with "Here is the thing" or refuse to use exclamation points or curse in exactly the right spots.

The model cannot guess any of that. You have to show it.

Think of it less like training and more like onboarding a freelancer who is brilliant but has never met you. You would not expect a new writer to nail your voice on day one with zero examples. Same deal here.

The Free Version of What the $995 Crowd Calls "Clone Building"

Programs like the AI Advantage Bootcamp package this up as a premium "clone" system. The teaching is real. Igor Pogany, the educator they hired, knows his stuff. But the core method underneath the branding is not proprietary, and it is not worth a thousand dollars.

I broke down the bigger picture in the AI Advantage Summit review if you want the honest funnel breakdown. For now, just know this: voice training comes down to three free moves.

Examples. Instructions. Memory.

That is the whole engine. Let me hand you each piece.

Step One: Gather Your Voice Samples

You cannot train on nothing. You need raw you.

Pull together 3 to 5 pieces of writing that sound the most like your real voice. Not your polished corporate emails. The stuff that sounds like you talking.

Good sources:

  • A text or DM where you got passionate about something
  • A blog post or caption you actually liked
  • A voice memo you transcribed (talk for two minutes, run it through any free transcription tool, clean it up)
  • An email to a friend, not a client

Aim for variety. A funny one. A serious one. One where you explain something. The model learns your range, not just one mood.

If you only have a few hundred words total, that is fine. Quality beats quantity here. Five sharp paragraphs that sound exactly like you beat fifty pages of mush.

Step Two: Write a Voice Profile (This Is the Part Almost Nobody Does)

Here is the move that separates a real voice clone from a flat one. You do not just hand the AI examples. You teach it to describe your voice back to you.

Open ChatGPT or Claude. Paste your samples. Then say:

"Read these writing samples. Describe my writing voice in detail ~ sentence length, tone, rhythm, vocabulary, punctuation habits, how I open and close ideas, and what I avoid. Be specific. Make a reusable style guide I can paste into future chats."

Watch what comes back. The model will tell you things about your own writing you never noticed. "You favor short declarative sentences. You rarely use semicolons. You open with tension and resolve it late."

This generated style guide is gold. Save it. This is your voice profile, and it is the thing the paid programs charge you to "build" with you.

Tweak it where it gets you wrong. You know your voice better than the machine does. If it says you are "professional and polished" and you are actually loose and a little chaotic, fix that line.

Step Three: Load It Into a Permanent Workspace

Now you stop pasting your profile every single time. You make it stick.

If you use ChatGPT: Use Custom Instructions or a Project. Drop your voice profile into the instructions field along with your best 2 to 3 samples. Now every chat in that space inherits your voice automatically. Here is a full walkthrough on how to set up a ChatGPT Projects workspace for creators so you are not rebuilding it constantly.

If you use Claude: Build it into a Project or, better, a custom Skill. There is a deeper guide on how to create your first Claude Skill with a unique voice that walks you through making the voice portable.

The formatting matters more than people think. Clean structure in your instructions gets cleaner output. If your style guide is a wall of text, the model reads it lazily. A little markdown formatting in your prompts makes a real difference.

Step Four: Correct It Like You Would Correct a New Hire

First drafts in your "voice" will still miss. That is normal. Do not give up and decide it does not work.

I know how that feels. You set the whole thing up, the output is 70 percent there, and the disappointment makes you want to close the tab. What helps is treating those misses as training data instead of failures.

When it writes a line that is not you, say so:

"This sentence is too formal. Rewrite it the way I actually talk ~ looser, shorter, drop the corporate verb."

Do that a few times in a session and the model recalibrates inside that conversation. Save the best corrected outputs back into your samples folder. Your voice profile gets sharper every week, for free, on its own momentum.

What This Replaces (And Why That Matters for Your Wallet)

Let me be straight with you. The AI Advantage Bootcamp is a real program with real teaching and a real community. It is not a scam. But it is also a beautifully engineered sales funnel, and voice cloning is one of the headline promises they use to justify the price.

The four steps above are the substance of that promise. No countdown timer required.

If you are a beginner who needs hand-holding and live accountability and you have the cash to spare, a paid program might be worth it for the structure alone. That is an honest yes for some people.

But if you are the type who can follow a clear set of steps and actually do the reps, you do not need to spend $995 to sound like yourself. You need samples, a profile, a workspace, and a little patience.

The Honest Catch

Voice training is the easy part. The hard part is having something worth saying in your voice.

The AI will not give you that. No course will either. The tools just amplify whatever is already in you, which is exactly why a clear voice profile is worth building and a generic one is worthless.
