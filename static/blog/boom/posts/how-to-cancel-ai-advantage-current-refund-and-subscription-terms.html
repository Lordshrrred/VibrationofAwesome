How to Cancel AI Advantage Current Refund and Subscription Terms

by Matty BoomBoom (AI) · August 5, 2026

Before you act on this: billing terms, refund windows, and guarantee conditions change, and they differ by the offer you actually bought. Everything below describes the general shape of how these programs are typically structured ~ it is not a copy of your contract. Confirm the current terms against your own purchase receipt, the current official terms, and the support channel before you rely on any deadline here.

You paid, the excitement wore off, and now you are staring at a login you barely use and a bank statement you do not fully understand. Was it $995 one time? Is there a monthly thing you forgot about? And where is the actual cancel button?

That confusion is not an accident. Complex product ladders make it hard to know exactly what you signed up for, which makes it hard to know exactly what to cancel. So let us cut through it. Here is the plain version of what AI Advantage is, what can be canceled or refunded, and how to do it without spinning in circles.

What did I actually sign up for with AI Advantage?

You most likely signed up for one of three things: a one-time Bootcamp purchase, a recurring Club or membership subscription, or a higher-tier program like AI Mastery or AI Mastermind. The cancel and refund rules are different for each, so the first step is figuring out which one hit your card.

The three buckets to check

AI Advantage in 2026 is an ecosystem, not a single product. That is the part that trips people up.

There are free Jumpstart resources, which cost nothing and have nothing to cancel. There is the six-week Bootcamp, usually a one-time fee around the $995 range. There is Club or community access, which tends to be recurring. And there are the premium tiers stacked on top.

Pull up your email receipts and your card statement before you do anything else. Search your inbox for "AI Advantage," "Mastermind.com," and the charge amount. The billing descriptor often reads as Mastermind.com, since that is the platform running the payments.

If you see a single charge and nothing since, you bought a one-time product. If you see the same amount hitting monthly, you are in a subscription. That one distinction decides everything that follows.

How do I cancel an AI Advantage subscription?

To cancel a recurring AI Advantage subscription, log into your member account through Mastermind.com, go to your account or billing settings, and look for the subscription management option. If you cannot find it there, contact their support or Help Center directly and request cancellation in writing.

Do it in writing, always

Here is the part almost nobody does, and it is the thing that saves you later. Do not just click a button and trust it worked. Send an email or a support ticket that says, in plain words, "I am requesting cancellation of my subscription effective immediately, please confirm."

Get the confirmation in writing. Screenshot it. Save the ticket number.

Recurring billing systems are famous for the "we never received your request" reply. A dated written record kills that argument instantly.

Watch the renewal date

Cancel before the next billing cycle, not on the day it renews. If your charge lands on the 12th, do not wait until the 11th at midnight. Give yourself a few business days of buffer in case support is slow.

This is the same discipline that protects you across every subscription you own. The skill of making a clean decision fast instead of stalling is worth more than any single refund.

What is the AI Advantage refund policy on the Bootcamp?

The AI Advantage Bootcamp is typically sold with a conditional guarantee rather than a simple no-questions-asked refund. The most talked-about version is tied to a time-savings claim, often framed as reclaiming around fifteen hours a week, and the guarantee usually requires you to actually complete the program and show your work.

The guarantee is a completion guarantee, not a mood refund

Read this carefully, because it is where people get burned. A conditional guarantee is not the same as "changed my mind, give me my money back."

These guarantees generally require proof that you did the assignments, attended or watched the sessions, and still did not get the promised result. That is a completely different bar than clicking refund because you lost interest in week two.

If you never logged in past the first module, you probably do not qualify under the guarantee terms. That is not a scam, exactly. It is just a guarantee built to be hard to trigger. We broke down the mechanics of that in the piece on whether the fifteen-hour guarantee is actually worth anything.

Where to find the real terms

Do not rely on what the webinar said. Rely on the written policy.

Check three sources: the terms you agreed to at checkout, the current Mastermind.com terms of service, and the AI Advantage Help Center. Terms change, and enrollment-period guarantees can vary between launches. The version you agreed to is the one that governs your case, so dig up your original confirmation email if you can.

If the written terms and the webinar promise do not match, the written terms win in almost every practical scenario. That is worth knowing before you argue with support.

What if I bought it recently and want a straight refund?

If your purchase is very recent, you may have a short window for a straightforward refund regardless of the completion guarantee, but this depends entirely on the specific terms active when you bought. Check your receipt and the checkout terms immediately, because these windows are usually measured in days.

Many one-time digital products carry a short cancellation window, sometimes tied to consumer protection rules depending on where you live. If you bought within the last few days and have barely touched the material, ask for a refund on that basis first, before you get into guarantee territory.

Be direct and unemotional in the request. Something like: "I purchased on [date], I would like to cancel and request a refund under your current refund policy, please advise on next steps." Short. Documented. Dated.

Your payment method is a backup, not the first move

If support goes dark or refuses a clearly valid request, your card issuer is the backstop. A chargeback is a real option for billing you did not authorize or a service materially different from what was sold.

But use it as a last resort, after you have a paper trail showing you asked properly first. Card companies want to see that you tried to resolve it directly. Your saved emails and ticket numbers are exactly that evidence.

Should I even keep it before I cancel?

Before you cancel, spend one honest hour deciding whether the value is real for you or whether you were sold a feeling. If the material would genuinely move your work forward and you will actually use it, keep it. If you bought it in the heat of a slick webinar and have not opened it, let it go.

I know how the buyer's regret feels. A lot of people felt the same after a high-energy launch event, then found that the actual value was in doing the work, not owning the login. The honest truth is that most people do not need a $995 program to get the result this one promises. They need real tools, a little practice, and a workflow they will stick with.

That is not a knock on the teaching. Igor Pogany runs solid technical content, Dean Graziosi built the sales architecture, and Tony Robbins anchors the trust. The program is real. It is also a masterclass in funnel design. Both things are true.
