Twenty Years of Trying (And What I Was Actually Doing Wrong)

March 1, 2026
by Matt EarthStar
8 min read

Twenty years.

That's how long I've been trying to build something real outside of a job I didn't want to be at.

Not twenty years of internet marketing. That's not the honest version. The honest version is twenty years of trying different things, failing at most of them, picking myself up, and trying something else ~ while still having to clock in every morning to a job that had nothing to do with what I actually wanted to build.

The first decade was heavy on internet marketing. SEO, email lists, affiliate stuff, sales pages. I studied it obsessively. I could talk strategy with anyone in the room. The problem was never the knowledge.

Then came crypto. I went deep ~ not just buying and holding, actually studying it. Market cycles, trading psychology, the tools. I learned a lot. I also lost real money. Some of it to bad decisions under pressure. Some of it to a platform called Celsius that froze withdrawals in 2022 and filed for bankruptcy while a lot of people ~ including me ~ watched money they'd parked there just disappear.

I'm not trading anymore. That chapter is closed.

Through all of it I still had to go to work. Every morning. Job I didn't love. Clock in. Clock out. Come home and keep building anyway.

That's the real story. Not a marketing veteran. A student of life who failed his ass off consistently while refusing to stop.

The Thing I Was Actually Doing Wrong

When I was in the internet marketing world, the playbook says: find someone killing it, model what they're doing, apply it to your niche.

This sounds reasonable. In practice it produces a universe of people who all sound exactly the same ~ including me for a long time.

I spent years writing content that sounded like everyone else's content. Using frameworks from courses. Building things from templates. Creating stuff that was a slight variation on stuff that was already everywhere.

It didn't feel fake in the moment. It felt like learning the craft. But what I know now is that I was suppressing the actual thing. Every formula I followed was another layer of insulation between me and the people who might genuinely connect with what I have to say.

Which, it turns out, is not a slightly better version of ten other people's content.

The Algorithm Trap

The second mistake ran alongside the first one: I was always chasing the platform instead of building something the platform couldn't touch.

Whatever Facebook was rewarding in 2014, I was doing that. Whatever YouTube liked in 2018, I was trying to fit that box. Every algorithm shift sent me into a recalibration spiral. What do I need to change? What's working now?

That is an exhausting way to operate. And it's completely backwards.

Algorithms are trying to figure out what humans want. The humans are already there. Build something humans genuinely want and the algorithm eventually finds it. Reverse-engineer the algorithm first and you're always one update away from starting over.

I know. Obvious in retrospect.

What Finally Cracked It Open

Two things happened in the last couple years that changed how I operate.

First, I stopped pretending my story was cleaner than it is. The crypto losses. The Celsius thing. The decade-plus of trying different angles and coming up short. The jobs I hated. All of it. Owning that story instead of packaging myself as someone who had a cleaner path ~ that was the shift.

Second, AI. Specifically Claude. Not to generate content for me. To think with me. To help me organize what I actually believe, examine my own thinking, and move from idea to execution faster than I ever could alone.

I've built the entire Vibration of Awesome ecosystem in the last year or so ~ the blog, the art store, AURA, the EarthStar Command Center that automates my video syndication pipeline ~ while still working a day job. That would not have been possible without these tools. The gap between vision and execution finally closed enough to actually build the thing.

Where I've Landed

The audience you want already exists. They're looking for someone who thinks the way you actually think, talks the way you actually talk, and has been through something real.

They are not looking for a polished version of you that sounds like everyone else who read the same playbooks.

The work isn't figuring out what to say. The work is stopping the filtering ~ the constant self-editing that keeps the real thing from coming through.

Twenty years of trying taught me that. It was long enough to learn it.

I'm still learning it.

~ Matt EarthStar
