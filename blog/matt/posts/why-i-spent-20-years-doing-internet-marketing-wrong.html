Why I Spent 20 Years Doing Internet Marketing Wrong

by Matt EarthStar February 28, 2026

I built funnels that converted. I wrote copy that sold. I followed every guru playbook in existence. And for 20 years I was completely missing the point.

Let me be clear: I made money. I learned real skills. I built things that worked. So this isn't one of those posts where someone admits they failed for years and now they have the secret. This is something else. This is about what I was actually doing while I was "succeeding."

The Hustle That Wasn't Mine

From day one, I was modeling other people. Not in the "study what works" way — in the "completely suppress your own instincts and copy the template" way. Someone told me the money was in email lists. I built email lists. Someone said write long-form sales pages. I wrote them, word for word following frameworks that weren't mine.

I got good at it. That's the trap. When you get good at something that isn't you, you double down instead of questioning it. Every win reinforced the wrong thing.

"Getting good at someone else's game is still playing the wrong game."

The real problem wasn't that the tactics didn't work. The problem was that I was building a version of internet marketing that felt hollow every single day — and I kept telling myself that was just the price of business.

What I Was Actually Good At

Here's the thing about spending 20 years grinding at something: you eventually figure out what you actually like doing. Not what converts. What lights you up.

For me it was always the creative side. The weird angle nobody else was taking. The honest piece of writing that said the uncomfortable thing. The music metaphor that shouldn't work in a marketing context but somehow did.

I'm a musician. I've been making music under EarthStar and Ruzindla for years. And the way I approach music — unconventional, emotionally raw, technically precise — is exactly how I should have been approaching marketing from day one. I just didn't have permission to do that. Or I thought I didn't.

The Permission Problem

Nobody in internet marketing was doing what I naturally wanted to do. The community rewarded proven templates, split-tested subject lines, and "what the data says." There was no room for "this is weird but it's true and I think it matters."

So I didn't do that. I did what the room rewarded.

Twenty years in, I look back and realize: the people who broke through and built something genuinely interesting were the ones who ignored the room. They brought their full, weird, specific self to the work. And the market found them — not because they optimized for the market, but because they were the only ones saying what they were saying.

What I'm Doing Differently Now

I'm writing the way I actually think. I'm building systems that fit my brain instead of retrofitting my brain into systems. The Forest Temple — my personal operating system — exists because no productivity method I found actually matched how I live.

The marketing I'm doing now is honest marketing. If something feels fake, I don't publish it. If a funnel requires me to be someone I'm not, I kill the funnel. This is not the "authentic marketing" buzzword approach. This is a practical decision: I've spent too many years building things that didn't feel like mine, and I'm done doing that.

The results so far? Slower in some ways. Clearer in every way that matters.

If you've been grinding at something for years and it keeps working but keeps feeling wrong, I'm not here to tell you to burn it down. But I am here to say: that feeling is data. Don't ignore it for another 20 years.

This blog is where I write about what's actually happening — with the music, the marketing, the systems, the weirdness. No framework. No funnel. Just the real version.
