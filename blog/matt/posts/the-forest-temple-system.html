The Forest Temple System: How I Organize My Life and Why It Works

by Matt EarthStar February 14, 2026

Everyone's selling you a productivity system. GTD, Notion templates, the second brain, the one thing. I tried most of them. Here's what I actually built for myself — and why none of the off-the-shelf stuff fit.

I have a lot going on. Music production under two projects (EarthStar and Ruzindla). Digital content work. Apple tech. A blog. A whole inner life that doesn't neatly slot into project categories. The standard systems weren't built for people like me. They were built for corporate workers who need to manage tasks. I'm not managing tasks. I'm managing a creative life.

Why Existing Systems Failed Me

GTD is great if your work is mostly reactive — processing inboxes, capturing tasks, clearing your head. But I'm not reactive. I'm generative. The problem isn't remembering what to do. The problem is choosing what to actually build from the infinite things I could build.

The "second brain" idea — building a knowledge base in Notion or Obsidian — is useful in theory. In practice, I kept building the system instead of using it. The system became the project. That's a trap I've fallen into too many times.

"A system that requires more maintenance than it saves you is a hobby, not a tool."

The "one thing" methodology works for people with one thing. I have many things, and they're not in conflict — they're in conversation with each other. My music informs how I write. My marketing knowledge informs how I present the music. My spiritual practice informs everything. Forcing singular focus was costing me the integration that makes my work interesting.

The Forest Temple Concept

The name came before the system. I live in a place that feels like a forest temple to me — not literally, but spiritually. It's a place of practice, of depth, of things that grow slowly. That feeling is what I wanted the system to carry.

The Forest Temple system has three core zones:

The Grove — Active Projects

Things I'm actively building right now. Not a to-do list. A small collection of living projects (never more than five) that are getting real attention. The Grove changes slowly and intentionally. Projects don't get added because I had an idea. They get added when the old thing is done or when something clearly has to move forward now.

The Archive — Completed and Dormant Work

Everything that's done or waiting. No judgment. No "I should be working on this." Just storage. The Archive doesn't call to me. It's just there. This was a huge mental health upgrade — I stopped feeling guilty about things I wasn't actively doing once they had a home that wasn't "the list."

The Temple — Practice and Ritual

The non-project stuff. Daily creative practice. Musical improvisation. Journaling. Meditation. Things that aren't "productive" in the task-completion sense but that make everything else work. The Temple is protected. It doesn't get sacrificed for the Grove. Ever.

What Makes It Work

It works because it matches how I actually think. I'm not fighting the system. I'm not trying to be a different kind of person. The Forest Temple is built around the fact that I'm a creative person with a spiritual practice who also needs to run a business — not a business person who also does creative stuff on the side.

The other thing that makes it work: it's mostly analog. I use a physical notebook. Minimal apps. The system lives in me, not in software I have to maintain.

I'll write more about the specifics — the weekly review, how the Temple sessions work, what the Grove selection criteria look like — in future posts. This is the overview. The thing I want you to take away is:

If the systems you've tried haven't worked, it might not be a discipline problem. It might be that the systems weren't built for the kind of person you are. Build the system that fits you. It takes time. It's worth it.
