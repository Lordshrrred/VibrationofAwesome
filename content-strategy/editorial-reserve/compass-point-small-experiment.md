# Turn Your Compass Point Result Into a Small Life Experiment

You do not need to reorganize your whole life because a reflection brought one neglected desire into focus. The next useful move can be much smaller: make time for a conversation, try a skill, or notice which part of your week deserves a different arrangement.

The [Compass Point](/tools/the-compass-point/) offers five questions and a Direction Card. It names a direction based on your choices. Use it to frame an experiment you can afford to try, rather than asking it to make a consequential decision for you.

## What can a Direction Card actually tell you?

It can reflect the season, interests, and constraints you selected. It cannot know everything about your responsibilities, finances, relationships, or health.

That distinction makes the tool more useful. A direction such as wanting more connection can become a concrete invitation to someone you want to spend time with. It does not require you to conclude that everything else in your life is wrong.

Before you start, write one sentence about what prompted the question. “My week is full, but I keep postponing making music” gives you something to return to after the result.

## Step 1: Which season describes your current situation?

The tool offers building, rebuilding, waiting, searching, and settled but restless. Choose the closest fit for today, even if another season sounds more appealing.

Your choice is a way to consider the situation, not a permanent identity. Someone rebuilding after a change may need an experiment with less commitment than someone who already has a stable routine and wants to add a creative practice.

Do not use a category to erase responsibilities. A direction has to fit into the life you are actually living before it can change that life.

## Step 2: Where does your attention keep returning?

The next questions ask where your energy wants to go and what has been pulling at you. Answer with the interest or tension you have actually noticed.

You might want more time with people, space for making something, stillness, progress toward a goal, or distance from an arrangement that no longer fits. Notice the difference between moving toward a concrete possibility and wanting relief from a difficult week.

Both deserve attention, but they suggest different experiments. An introduction to a local music group tests a specific possibility. Quitting every existing commitment is not a small experiment.

## Step 3: What does the direction require?

Choose the constraint that feels most relevant: time, courage, permission, clarity, or support. Then translate it into something observable.

If you need time, identify a window you can protect without pretending another responsibility disappears. If you need clarity, choose a conversation or a small trial that would answer one question. If you need support, name the kind of help and who might reasonably provide it.

The point is to make a requirement specific enough to work with. “I need to be a completely different person first” is not a practical preparation step.

## Step 4: How do you design a low-cost experiment?

Write down one action, a boundary, and a review date. For example: attend one open rehearsal next week, commit to nothing beyond that evening, and write down afterward whether you want a second visit.

For a quieter direction, an experiment could be one short walk without adding another podcast. For a skill you have neglected, it might be one practice session with the equipment you already own. Choose something reversible that produces information.

[Stanford's Life Design Lab](https://lifedesignlab.stanford.edu/dyl) describes prototyping and testing possibilities as part of its life-design approach. This particular exercise is VOA's practical adaptation, not a validated assessment or a promise of a particular outcome.

## What should you review afterward?

Ask what happened, what surprised you, and whether another attempt makes sense. Separate liking an idea from enjoying its ordinary practice. Either answer is useful.

You can change direction without declaring the experiment a failure. Learning that a possibility does not fit can save you a much larger commitment later.

## FAQ

**Should I act on the result immediately?**

You can take a small reversible step. Give major financial, work, health, or relationship decisions the information and support they require.

**What if I get a result that does not feel right?**

Keep your judgment. Review the choices you made or set the result aside. A reflection tool does not overrule you.

**Where can I explore the idea further?**

Visit the [Purpose hub](/hubs/purpose/) or open the [Field Guide](/field-guide/). Start with one experiment you can learn from; let the larger story develop from what you discover.
